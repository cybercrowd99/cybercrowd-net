# ACOUSTIC_PHASE_ALIGNMENT_REGULATOR (APAR)

## 1. Purpose
The Acoustic Phase‑Alignment Regulator eliminates phase drift between acoustic waves. It ensures all vibrational modes remain phase‑locked and coherent.

## 2. Phase Field Definition
Define the phase‑drift field Pₐ as:
Pₐ = Σ |φ_i − φ̄|

where φ_i is the phase of each acoustic mode and φ̄ is the manifold‑wide mean phase.

Pₐ measures how misaligned the phases of acoustic waves are.

## 3. Stability Condition
The manifold is phase‑stable when:
Pₐ → 0
meaning all acoustic waves share a unified phase reference.

## 4. Alignment Flow
To eliminate phase drift, apply:
∂Pₐ/∂t = −θPₐ
where θ controls how quickly phase alignment is restored.

## 5. Phase‑Linked Instability Prevention
APAR prevents:
- phase‑offset resonance  
- drifting interference patterns  
- unstable beat‑envelopes  
- long‑term vibrational misalignment  
- cumulative acoustic fatigue  

## 6. Integration Pipeline
APAR integrates with:
- HCIE to ensure no harmonic branching disrupts phase alignment  
- CFMS to prevent cross‑frequency drift  
- SWNM to stop standing waves from locking in phase offsets  
- HCL to absorb distortions released during phase correction  

## 7. Sovereign Logic Summary
Harmonic cascades interrupted → APAR aligns phases → No drift → Geometry becomes phase‑coherent
