CyberCrowd Rules & Regulations

This document describes how CyberCrowd operates as a sovereign creative ecosystem. It is written for creators, viewers, operators, and partners who need to understand the lanes, the systems, and the boundaries.

1. Purpose of This Document

CyberCrowd is not a traditional “platform.” It is a value cycle and a world engine designed to keep power, credit, and compensation close to the people who actually create and participate.

The purpose of this document is to define:

  • How creators, viewers, and operators interact inside CyberCrowd.
  • How systems like WDIG and adWorm behave and what they are allowed to do.
  • What is protected, what is prohibited, and what is non‑negotiable in the value cycle.

These rules are not written to sound like corporate legalese. They are written to be readable, enforceable, and aligned with CyberCrowd’s doctrine: no extraction, no hidden games, no bait‑and‑switch.

2. Core Principles of CyberCrowd

2.1 Value Cycle Integrity

The value cycle is the closed loop that keeps energy, attention, and money circulating inside CyberCrowd. When someone creates, watches, reacts, or contributes signal, that activity should benefit the ecosystem, not an external ad network or data broker.

CyberCrowd commits to:

  • No external siphoning of behavioral data for third‑party monetization.
  • No dark funnels where value leaves the system without a clear reason.
  • Traceable flows of compensation and rewards, so participants can see how value moves.

2.2 Creator Sovereignty

Creators inside CyberCrowd are treated as operators, not as disposable content suppliers. They control their lanes, their presence, and their relationship with their audience.

Creator sovereignty means:

  • Lane control: Operators decide what runs in their lane, within safety and legal boundaries.
  • No forced algorithmic reshaping: The system does not silently rewrite or bury their work.
  • Clear upgrade paths: Top creators can become beta operators, testers, and co‑designers of new features.

2.3 Public Participation

The public is not a passive audience. Viewers can become signal contributors, co‑broadcasters, and participants in events through WDIG and other systems.

Public participation includes:

  • Attaching cams (phone, drone, VR, etc.) into live events.
  • Providing feedback that actually influences features and lanes.
  • Earning rewards for meaningful participation, not just for being tracked.

2.4 No Billion‑Dollar Exit

CyberCrowd is not designed as a flip or a quick exit vehicle. The system is built as infrastructure that should outlive any single founder, investor, or hype cycle.

This means:

  • Reinvestment first: Surplus is directed back into tools, lanes, and community support.
  • No “growth at any cost” mandate: Stability and integrity outrank vanity metrics.
  • Long‑term stewardship: Governance is oriented toward continuity, not liquidation.

2.5 Transparent Systems

CyberCrowd systems are allowed to be complex, but not allowed to be mysterious on purpose. Participants should be able to understand, at a high level, how decisions are made.

Transparency includes:

  • Clear rules for how lanes are surfaced, recommended, or prioritized.
  • Visible logs for ad events, enforcement actions, and major system changes.
  • No shadow throttling: If reach is limited, there is a reason and a record.

2.6 Safety Without Censorship

CyberCrowd protects people and lanes from harm without turning into a blunt censorship machine. Safety is lane‑based, contextual, and focused on preventing real damage.

Safety means:

  • Blocking harm, not ideas: Threats, harassment, and exploitation are not allowed.
  • Context‑aware enforcement: Systems and humans look at patterns, not isolated words.
  • Appeal paths: Operators can challenge enforcement decisions through defined channels.

3. System Components

3.1 WDIG – The Live Engine

WDIG is the real‑time broadcast spine of CyberCrowd. It connects creators, viewers, and multiple camera sources into synchronized live events. WDIG is where the crowd can literally “plug in” to the same moment.

3.1.1 Signal Acceptance

When a viewer attaches a camera to a WDIG event, they become a signal contributor. Their feed may be used, mixed, or highlighted by the operator.

Rules for signal acceptance:

  • Consent: Viewers must explicitly opt in to attach their cam to an event.
  • Revocability: Viewers can disconnect at any time; the system must honor it quickly.
  • Clarity: It must be clear when a viewer’s feed is live, recorded, or queued.

3.1.2 Operator Priority

The operator (creator) controls the lane. WDIG does not override operator choices with automated decisions about which cams to show, except in safety‑critical situations.

Operator priority includes:

  • Choosing which cams to feature, mute, or drop.
  • Setting event rules for what kind of signal is acceptable.
  • Pausing or ending the event when necessary.

3.1.3 Multi‑Cam Protocol

WDIG supports multiple camera types: phones, drones, VR rigs, 3D cams, and more. To keep events coherent, all cams must follow synchronization and quality rules.

Multi‑cam rules:

  • Time sync: Cams must align to the WDIG time spine; extreme drift may be dropped.
  • Quality thresholds: Extremely low‑quality or corrupted feeds may be rejected.
  • Safety filters: Feeds may be auto‑flagged if they appear to show prohibited content.

3.1.4 Viewer Participation Rights

Viewers who contribute signal have rights:

  • Right to withdraw: They can stop contributing at any time.
  • Right to clarity: They should know how their feed might be used in the event.
  • Right to respect: Operators may not use contributed cams to harass or humiliate contributors.

3.1.5 Latency & Sync Rules

WDIG maintains a stable time spine so that all participants share a coherent event. Some delay is inevitable, but extreme desync is not acceptable.

Latency rules:

  • Target latency windows are defined per event type.
  • Out‑of‑range feeds may be delayed, buffered, or dropped to preserve event integrity.
  • System transparency: Operators can see basic latency and sync status for their event.

3.2 adWorm – The Ethical Advertising Engine

adWorm is CyberCrowd’s monetization system. It is explicitly designed to avoid predatory, surveillance‑based advertising. It connects brands, creators, and viewers without turning anyone into a product.

3.2.1 No Behavioral Extraction

adWorm does not build invasive behavioral profiles. It does not follow people across the internet, and it does not sell personal data to third parties.

This means:

  • No cross‑site tracking pixels for external ad networks.
  • No hidden fingerprinting to identify devices or users.
  • No sale of personal data as a business model.

3.2.2 Creator Revenue Share

When adWorm runs an ad in or around a creator’s lane, that creator is treated as an operator and receives a defined share of the revenue.

Revenue share principles:

  • Clear formulas: The share model is documented and not arbitrarily changed in secret.
  • Event‑based accounting: Each ad event is logged and attributable.
  • Fair weighting: High‑value participation and stable lanes are rewarded appropriately.

3.2.3 Crowd Rewards

Viewers may receive rewards for participating in ad experiences that are clearly labeled and optional. Rewards are for voluntary engagement, not for being tracked.

  • Opt‑in only: No forced ad interactions to access basic functionality.
  • Transparent value: Viewers know what they receive for participating.
  • No manipulative loops: No endless “watch more to unlock basic rights” mechanics.

3.2.4 Ad Placement Integrity

adWorm placements must be honest and non‑deceptive. Ads cannot masquerade as user content or system messages.

  • Clear labeling: Ads are visibly marked as ads.
  • No forced interruptions that break critical moments in live events without warning.
  • No deceptive overlays: Ads cannot hide controls or mislead users into clicking.

3.2.5 Transparency Ledger

adWorm maintains a ledger of ad events. While not every detail is public, the existence and volume of ad activity in a lane or event can be inspected at a high level.

  • Logged events: Each ad impression, click, or interaction is recorded.
  • Operator visibility: Creators can see how ads ran around their content.
  • Auditability: The system can be audited for compliance with these rules.

3.3 The Value Cycle

The value cycle is the economic circulatory system of CyberCrowd. It defines how contribution turns into reward, how creators become operators, and how the public fuels discovery without being exploited.

3.3.1 Contribution → Reward

Every meaningful action inside CyberCrowd should have a potential return. Not every action is paid, but the system recognizes and can reward contribution.

  • Creators: Earn through lanes, events, and adWorm participation.
  • Viewers: Earn through participation credits, event bonuses, or recognition.
  • Operators: Earn through deeper system roles, testing, and governance tasks.

3.3.2 Creator → Operator Path

Top creators can be invited into operator roles. As operators, they help test new features, shape WDIG and adWorm, and receive compensation for that work.

  • Eligibility: Based on stability, contribution, and alignment with core principles.
  • Compensation: Defined beta‑tester or operator pay structures.
  • Influence: Operators can provide structured feedback that affects system design.

3.3.3 Public → Engine Path

The public fuels discovery by watching, reacting, and contributing signal. CyberCrowd uses this activity to surface lanes and events, not to build invasive profiles.

  • Aggregate patterns: The system looks at crowd‑level behavior, not individual dossiers.
  • Discovery without stalking: Recommendations are based on in‑system activity, not external tracking.
  • Respect for choice: People can tune or reset their discovery experience.

3.3.4 Reinvestment Doctrine

CyberCrowd reinvests into itself. Infrastructure, tools, and community support are prioritized over extraction.

  • Tooling: Building better lanes, better WDIG capabilities, and better creator tools.
  • Support: Funding moderation, safety, and education for operators and viewers.
  • Expansion: Opening new creative domains (music, comedy, VR, etc.) as the crowd grows.

3.3.5 Anti‑Extraction Clause

CyberCrowd may not be converted into a pure ad‑farm or data‑harvesting operation. Any attempt to pivot the system into extraction violates its founding doctrine.

  • No pivot to surveillance: System changes cannot introduce hidden tracking as a core model.
  • No hostile governance shift: Governance changes that break this clause are invalid.
  • Community alarm: Major deviations must be surfaced to operators and the crowd.

4. Conduct & Participation Rules

4.1 Creator Conduct

Creators are given powerful tools and lanes. With that comes responsibility to protect the crowd and the integrity of the system.

4.1.1 Respect the Crowd

Creators must treat viewers and signal contributors as people, not as disposable content.

  • No targeted harassment of individuals or groups.
  • No doxxing or exposure of private information.
  • No humiliation loops built around viewer cams or contributions.

4.1.2 WDIG Safety Protocols

When running WDIG events, creators must follow safety protocols designed to protect participants.

  • Content awareness: Do not knowingly broadcast illegal or extremely harmful content.
  • Emergency response: If a feed shows imminent harm, operators should pause and report.
  • Age awareness: Do not involve minors in adult‑themed events.

4.1.3 Lane Stability

Stable lanes are lanes that do not constantly crash, glitch, or become hostile environments.

  • Technical stability: Avoid intentionally breaking streams or sabotaging events.
  • Social stability: Do not cultivate chaos purely for harm or harassment.
  • Predictable rules: Communicate lane rules to participants when relevant.

4.1.4 Avoid Harmful Manipulation

Creators may not use CyberCrowd tools to manipulate viewers into self‑harm, financial scams, or other forms of exploitation.

  • No scams: Fraudulent schemes are prohibited.
  • No coercion: Do not pressure viewers into unsafe or illegal actions.
  • No deepfake abuse: Do not use synthetic media to deceive or harm individuals.

4.2 Viewer Conduct

Viewers are part of the engine. Their behavior affects the quality and safety of lanes and events.

4.2.1 Cam Submission Rules

When viewers attach cams to WDIG or other events, they must follow submission rules.

  • No illegal content: Do not stream crimes, exploitation, or extreme violence.
  • No non‑consensual capture: Do not stream people who have a reasonable expectation of privacy.
  • No malicious feeds: Do not intentionally send corrupted or harmful signal.

4.2.2 Respect Operator Control

Operators decide how their events run. Viewers must respect lane rules and operator decisions.

  • No hijacking attempts: Do not try to override or bypass operator controls.
  • No spam: Do not flood events with meaningless or disruptive signal.
  • Follow posted rules: If an event has specific guidelines, honor them.

4.2.3 Avoid Disruptive Behavior

Viewers should not intentionally degrade the experience for others.

  • No harassment: Do not target other viewers with abuse.
  • No hate speech: Do not use CyberCrowd lanes to promote hatred or violence.
  • No exploit attempts: Do not attempt to break or exploit the system.

4.3 Right to Refuse Service

CyberCrowd operators, creators, and system maintainers reserve the right to refuse service, participation, or access to any individual whose actions threaten system integrity, safety, stability, or violate core doctrine.

  • Integrity protection: Service may be refused to prevent system abuse or exploitation.
  • Safety enforcement: Service may be denied to individuals posing harm to others.
  • Doctrine alignment: Actions that violate CyberCrowd’s founding principles may result in refusal.

5. System Safety & Enforcement

5.1 Automated Enforcement

CyberCrowd uses lane‑based automated enforcement to protect the system without turning into a blind punishment engine.

5.1.1 Lane Integrity Checks

Automated checks monitor lanes for extreme instability, repeated violations, or technical abuse.

  • Rate limits: Prevent spam and overload.
  • Abuse detection: Flag patterns of harassment or exploitation.
  • Technical anomalies: Detect repeated crashes or malicious traffic.

5.1.2 Signal Verification

Some signals may be verified or lightly analyzed to prevent obvious abuse.

  • Basic content checks for clearly illegal material.
  • Integrity checks to ensure feeds are not corrupted or malicious.
  • Minimal inspection: Only what is necessary to enforce rules.

5.1.3 adWorm Compliance

Automated systems ensure that adWorm placements follow the ethical rules defined in this document.

  • Placement checks: Ensure ads are labeled and not deceptive.
  • Tracking checks: Prevent introduction of unauthorized tracking.
  • Ledger consistency: Confirm that logged events match actual activity.

5.2 Human Oversight

Human operators and moderators intervene when automated systems are not enough or when context is required.

  • Safety incidents: When there is risk of real‑world harm.
  • Lane instability: When a lane repeatedly becomes hostile or unusable.
  • Doctrine violations: When actions threaten the core principles of CyberCrowd.

Human decisions should be documented, reviewable, and consistent with this rule set.

6. Economic Rules

6.1 Creator Compensation

Creators and operators are compensated for their work, their stability, and their contribution to the ecosystem.

6.1.1 Operator Pay Structure

Operators may receive base compensation, performance‑based rewards, or event‑based payouts.

  • Clear tiers: Different roles and responsibilities have defined pay structures.
  • Performance metrics: Stability, engagement quality, and adherence to rules may factor in.
  • Review cycles: Pay structures can be updated, but not retroactively in a deceptive way.

6.1.2 Beta Tester Compensation

When creators act as beta testers for new features, they are compensated for their time and risk.

  • Defined scope: Each test has clear expectations and timelines.
  • Fair pay: Compensation reflects the effort and potential disruption.
  • Feedback channels: Testers have direct lines to report issues and suggestions.

6.1.3 Revenue Distribution

Revenue from adWorm and other monetization channels is distributed according to documented formulas.

  • Operator share: Creators receive a defined portion of revenue generated around their lanes.
  • System share: A portion is reserved for infrastructure and reinvestment.
  • Community share: Some revenue may support community tools, safety, and expansion.

6.2 Viewer Rewards

Viewers can earn rewards for meaningful participation, not for being passively observed.

6.2.1 Participation Credits

Participation credits may be granted for attending events, contributing signal, or supporting lanes.

  • Transparent rules: Viewers know what actions earn credits.
  • Non‑exploitative: Credits are not used to trap people in endless grind loops.
  • Usability: Credits can be used in ways that feel meaningful, not trivial.

6.2.2 Event Bonuses

Special events may offer bonuses for participation, such as limited‑time rewards or recognition.

  • Clear terms: Event rules are published in advance.
  • Fair access: Bonuses are not restricted to a tiny inner circle without reason.
  • No bait‑and‑switch: Promised bonuses must be delivered.

6.2.3 Non‑Monetary Recognition

Not all rewards are financial. Recognition, status, and visible appreciation matter.

  • Badges or markers for consistent, positive participation.
  • Highlight opportunities for standout contributors.
  • Community roles for trusted participants.

7. Legal & Compliance Layer

7.1 Age Requirements

CyberCrowd must comply with applicable laws regarding minors and age‑restricted content.

7.1.1 Minimum Age Rules

Minimum age requirements may vary by region and by feature. Some lanes or events may be restricted to adults.

  • Age gates: Certain content requires age confirmation.
  • Regional compliance: Local laws may impose stricter rules.
  • Operator responsibility: Creators must not knowingly involve minors in adult content.

7.1.2 Minor Protection Protocols

When minors are present on the system, additional protections apply.

  • No exploitation: Any exploitation of minors is strictly prohibited and reported.
  • Limited data: Minimal data is collected about minors, consistent with law.
  • Content filters: Minors should not be exposed to age‑inappropriate lanes.

7.2 Content Restrictions

CyberCrowd respects freedom of expression but must prohibit certain content to comply with law and protect people.

7.2.1 Prohibited Content

Prohibited content includes, but is not limited to:

  • Sexual exploitation or abuse of any person, especially minors.
  • Incitement to violence, terrorism, or organized crime.
  • Extremely graphic violence intended to shock or traumatize.
  • Content that clearly violates applicable law.

7.2.2 Sensitive Material Rules

Some content may be allowed but treated as sensitive and restricted.

  • Warning labels: Sensitive content may require explicit opt‑in.
  • Age restrictions: Some material is limited to adults.
  • Context checks: Enforcement considers context, not just keywords.

7.3 Data Handling

CyberCrowd aims to minimize data collection and avoid surveillance‑style practices.

7.3.1 No Behavioral Tracking

CyberCrowd does not build invasive behavioral profiles for sale or external ad targeting.

  • No third‑party trackers embedded for external ad networks.
  • No hidden profiling for resale or unrelated purposes.
  • Limited analytics: Only what is needed to run and improve the system.

7.3.2 Minimal Data Retention

Data is retained only as long as necessary for operations, safety, and legal compliance.

  • Retention limits: Logs and records have defined lifespans.
  • Secure storage: Data is stored with appropriate protections.
  • Deletion paths: Users may request deletion where legally allowed.

7.3.3 User Control

Users should have meaningful control over their data and experience.

  • Settings: Options to adjust discovery, privacy, and participation.
  • Export: Where possible, users can export key data.
  • Consent: Major changes to data use require clear communication.

8. Amendments & Future Expansion

CyberCrowd is a living system. As new technologies, lanes, and creative forms emerge, these rules may need to evolve.

Amendments may occur when:

  • New systems (e.g., new WDIG modes, new adWorm formats) are introduced.
  • New creative domains (e.g., advanced VR, mixed reality, AI‑assisted performance) come online.
  • New safety or legal requirements arise.
  • The value cycle expands in ways that require clearer definitions.

All significant changes to these rules should be:

  • Logged: Recorded in a public or semi‑public change log.
  • Communicated: Announced to creators and operators in clear language.
  • Aligned: Consistent with the core principles defined in Section 2.

CyberCrowd’s long‑term goal is simple: build an expansive, inclusive ecosystem where music, comedy, gaming, live broadcast, VR, and whatever comes next are shaped with the crowd, not imposed on them.
