CyberCrowd Rules & Regulations

This document describes how CyberCrowd operates as a sovereign creative ecosystem. It is written for creators, viewers, operators, and partners who need to understand the lanes, the systems, and the boundaries.

1. Purpose of This Document

CyberCrowd is not a traditional “platform.” It is a value cycle and a world engine designed to keep power, credit, and compensation close to the people who actually create and participate.

The purpose of this document is to define:

  • How creators, viewers, and operators interact inside CyberCrowd.
  • How systems like WDIG and adWorm behave and what they are allowed to do.
  • What is protected, what is prohibited, and what is non‑negotiable in the value cycle.

These rules are not written to sound like corporate legalese. They are written to be readable, enforceable, and aligned with CyberCrowd’s doctrine: no extraction, no hidden games, no bait‑and‑switch.

2. Core Principles of CyberCrowd

2.1 Value Cycle Integrity

The value cycle is the closed loop that keeps energy, attention, and money circulating inside CyberCrowd. When someone creates, watches, reacts, or contributes signal, that activity should benefit the ecosystem, not an external ad network or data broker.

CyberCrowd commits to:

  • No external siphoning of behavioral data for third‑party monetization.
  • No dark funnels where value leaves the system without a clear reason.
  • Traceable flows of compensation and rewards, so participants can see how value moves.

2.2 Creator Sovereignty

Creators inside CyberCrowd are treated as operators, not as disposable content suppliers. They control their lanes, their presence, and their relationship with their audience.

Creator sovereignty means:

  • Lane control: Operators decide what runs in their lane, within safety and legal boundaries.
  • No forced algorithmic reshaping: The system does not silently rewrite or bury their work.
  • Clear upgrade paths: Top creators can become beta operators, testers, and co‑designers of new features.

2.3 Public Participation

The public is not a passive audience. Viewers can become signal contributors, co‑broadcasters, and participants in events through WDIG and other systems.

Public participation includes:

  • Attaching cams (phone, drone, VR, etc.) into live events.
  • Providing feedback that actually influences features and lanes.
  • Earning rewards for meaningful participation, not just for being tracked.

2.4 No Billion‑Dollar Exit

CyberCrowd is not designed as a flip or a quick exit vehicle. The system is built as infrastructure that should outlive any single founder, investor, or hype cycle.

This means:

  • Reinvestment first: Surplus is directed back into tools, lanes, and community support.
  • No “growth at any cost” mandate: Stability and integrity outrank vanity metrics.
  • Long‑term stewardship: Governance is oriented toward continuity, not liquidation.

2.5 Transparent Systems

CyberCrowd systems are allowed to be complex, but not allowed to be mysterious on purpose. Participants should be able to understand, at a high level, how decisions are made.

Transparency includes:

  • Clear rules for how lanes are surfaced, recommended, or prioritized.
  • Visible logs for ad events, enforcement actions, and major system changes.
  • No shadow throttling: If reach is limited, there is a reason and a record.

2.6 Safety Without Censorship

CyberCrowd protects people and lanes from harm without turning into a blunt censorship machine. Safety is lane‑based, contextual, and focused on preventing real damage.

Safety means:

  • Blocking harm, not ideas: Threats, harassment, and exploitation are not allowed.
  • Context‑aware enforcement: Systems and humans look at patterns, not isolated words.
  • Appeal paths: Operators can challenge enforcement decisions through defined channels.

3. System Components

3.1 WDIG – The Live Engine

WDIG is the real‑time broadcast spine of CyberCrowd. It connects creators, viewers, and multiple camera sources into synchronized live events. WDIG is where the crowd can literally “plug in” to the same moment.

3.1.1 Signal Acceptance

When a viewer attaches a camera to a WDIG event, they become a signal contributor. Their feed may be used, mixed, or highlighted by the operator.

Rules for signal acceptance:

  • Consent: Viewers must explicitly opt in to attach their cam to an event.
  • Revocability: Viewers can disconnect at any time; the system must honor it quickly.
  • Clarity: It must be clear when a viewer’s feed is live, recorded, or queued.

3.1.2 Operator Priority

The operator (creator) controls the lane. WDIG does not override operator choices with automated decisions about which cams to show, except in safety‑critical situations.

Operator priority includes:

  • Choosing which cams to feature, mute, or drop.
  • Setting event rules for what kind of signal is acceptable.
  • Pausing or ending the event when necessary.

3.1.3 Multi‑Cam Protocol

WDIG supports multiple camera types: phones, drones, VR rigs, 3D cams, and more. To keep events coherent, all cams must follow synchronization and quality rules.

Multi‑cam rules:

  • Time sync: Cams must align to the WDIG time spine; extreme drift may be dropped.
  • Quality thresholds: Extremely low‑quality or corrupted feeds may be rejected.
  • Safety filters: Feeds may be auto‑flagged if they appear to show prohibited content.

3.1.4 Viewer Participation Rights

Viewers who contribute signal have rights:

  • Right to withdraw: They can stop contributing at any time.
  • Right to clarity: They should know how their feed might be used in the event.
  • Right to respect: Operators may not use contributed cams to harass or humiliate contributors.

3.1.5 Latency & Sync Rules

WDIG maintains a stable time spine so that all participants share a coherent event. Some delay is inevitable, but extreme desync is not acceptable.

Latency rules:

  • Target latency windows are defined per event type.
  • Out‑of‑range feeds may be delayed, buffered, or dropped to preserve event integrity.
  • System transparency: Operators can see basic latency and sync status for their event.

3.2 adWorm – The Ethical Advertising Engine

adWorm is CyberCrowd’s monetization system. It is explicitly designed to avoid predatory, surveillance‑based advertising. It connects brands, creators, and viewers without turning anyone into a product.

3.2.1 No Behavioral Extraction

adWorm does not build invasive behavioral profiles. It does not follow people across the internet, and it does not sell personal data to third parties.

This means:

  • No cross‑site tracking pixels for external ad networks.
  • No hidden fingerprinting to identify devices or users.
  • No sale of personal data as a business model.

3.2.2 Creator Revenue Share

When adWorm runs an ad in or around a creator’s lane, that creator is treated as an operator and receives a defined share of the revenue.

Revenue share principles:

  • Clear formulas: The share model is documented and not arbitrarily changed in secret.
  • Event‑based accounting: Each ad event is logged and attributable.
  • Fair weighting: High‑value participation and stable lanes are rewarded appropriately.

3.2.3 Crowd Rewards

Viewers may receive rewards for participating in ad experiences that are clearly labeled and optional. Rewards are for voluntary engagement, not for being tracked.

  • Opt‑in only: No forced ad interactions to access basic functionality.
  • Transparent value: Viewers know what they receive for participating.
  • No manipulative loops: No endless “watch more to unlock basic rights” mechanics.

3.2.4 Ad Placement Integrity

adWorm placements must be honest and non‑deceptive. Ads cannot masquerade as user content or system messages.

  • Clear labeling: Ads are visibly marked as ads.
  • No forced interruptions that break critical moments in live events without warning.
  • No deceptive overlays: Ads cannot hide controls or mislead users into clicking.

3.2.5 Transparency Ledger

adWorm maintains a ledger of ad events. While not every detail is public, the existence and volume of ad activity in a lane or event can be inspected at a high level.

  • Logged events: Each ad impression, click, or interaction is recorded.
  • Operator visibility: Creators can see how ads ran around their content.
  • Auditability: The system can be audited for compliance with these rules.

3.3 The Value Cycle

The value cycle is the economic circulatory system of CyberCrowd. It defines how contribution turns into reward, how creators become operators, and how the public fuels discovery without being exploited.

3.3.1 Contribution → Reward

Every meaningful action inside CyberCrowd should have a potential return. Not every action is paid, but the system recognizes and can reward contribution.

  • Creators: Earn through lanes, events, and adWorm participation.
  • Viewers: Earn through participation credits, event bonuses, or recognition.
  • Operators: Earn through deeper system roles, testing, and governance tasks.

3.3.2 Creator → Operator Path

Top creators can be invited into operator roles. As operators, they help test new features, shape WDIG and adWorm, and receive compensation for that work.

  • Eligibility: Based on stability, contribution, and alignment with core principles.
  • Compensation: Defined beta‑tester or operator pay structures.
  • Influence: Operators can provide structured feedback that affects system design.
