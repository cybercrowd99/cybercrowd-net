CyberMarket Local Inventory

How scanned items become local search.

Local Items Should Be Findable

CyberMarket Local Inventory helps make nearby items visible. When an item is scanned, photographed, priced, or observed on a shelf, that item may become a local inventory signal.

A local inventory signal can help people find what is actually nearby instead of only seeing distant online listings.

Inventory Purpose

The purpose of CyberMarket Local Inventory is local discovery. A product sitting in a nearby store should not be invisible just because it is not part of a large online marketplace.

A scanned item may carry useful context: barcode, shelf photo, price observation, store proximity, time seen, and availability observation.

Scan to Local Search

Scanned item ↓ Local inventory signal ↓ Nearby search result ↓ Customer discovery

Work Capture may capture the item. CyberMarket Local Inventory explains how that item can become visible to nearby search.

Barcode-to-Item Connection

A barcode can act as the item anchor. The barcode helps connect the physical shelf item to a product identity without requiring the worker to type the product name again and again.

The barcode alone is not the whole record. The local inventory signal may also need shelf context, store context, time context, price observation, and availability observation.

Shelf Photo Context

A shelf photo can show what the barcode cannot. It may show placement, condition, display quality, nearby products, signage, price tags, and whether the item was actually present at the time of capture.

The photo helps turn a product code into real-world local evidence.

Store Proximity

CyberMarket Local Inventory is about nearby discovery. A customer searching for an item may need to know whether that item was seen close enough to be useful.

Store proximity helps local stores, discount stores, salvage stores, small retailers, and other nearby sellers become visible when people are looking for real items close to them.

Price Observation

A price observation records what price was seen with the item. This is not the same as checkout, payment, or final sale authority.

A price observation helps searchers understand why a local item may be worth visiting, comparing, or checking before buying elsewhere.

Availability Observation

Availability observation means the item was seen in a local place at a certain time. It does not guarantee the item will remain available forever.

The signal should show that the item was observed, where it was observed, and when it was observed.

Local Search Result

A local search result may show that an item was seen nearby. The result may include item identity, store proximity, photo context, price observation, and time seen.

Example Local Result

A couch, tool, food item, display item, or discount product is scanned or photographed in a local store. The item becomes searchable nearby with its observed place, price, photo, and time seen.
