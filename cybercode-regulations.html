KAREN Rule / Counter-Thesis See-Saw

CyberCode uses a see-saw balance for public challenge. One side blocks weaponized noise. The other side rewards valuable clarity. CyberCrowd must not punish because someone screams, but it also must not ignore a person who reveals a flaw before harm occurs.

KAREN Rule / Anti Side-Slap: frivolous, false, performative, weaponized, ego-driven, or bad-faith complaints may not force CyberCrowd to draw enforcement against a person, profile, creator, client, reviewer, board member, or platform lane.
Counter-Thesis / Adversarial Value: valuable adversarial input may be elevated when it reveals a flaw, loophole, safety risk, litigation exposure, policy gap, public confusion, hidden harm, or preventable platform failure.
Noise goes down: CyberCrowd should not reward side slaps, profile attacks, pile-ons, outrage theater, competitive narrative, or complaint behavior used as a weapon.
Clarity goes up: CyberCrowd may reward a person who helps prevent harm, improve a rule, expose an escape clause, protect public trust, or help CyberCode avoid repeat blindness.
Reward loop: valuable input may earn a thank-you, credit, comp, membership time, service-call credit, CyberShop credit, Docket Forum credit, access, or recognition without buying control.
Saved Ping preservation: valuable adversarial input may become a Saved Ping, identity evidence, Water Decree support, CyberCode precedent, or platform memory.
CyberCrowd will not swing the sword just because someone screams.
CyberCrowd may reward the person who prevents harm before the sword is needed.
