CYBERCROWD FORENSIC CONTINUITY INCIDENT RESPONSE LAW V1
Incident response and forensic continuity doctrine for CyberCrowd. This document defines how continuity alarms, compromise events, transport anomalies, and security incidents are detected, contained, preserved, reviewed, and recovered.
FORENSIC-001
Core Rule
An incident must not erase truth. CyberCrowd incident response must: detect freeze contain preserve trace review recover without silently rewriting continuity history.
FORENSIC-002

FORENSIC-003
Alarm Topology
Alarms attach to continuity compartments. Alarm targets may include: - route - node - connector - transport lane - replay segment - migration chain - heartbeat chain - governance lane - archive lane The system should isolate the affected compartment, not collapse the entire continuity fabric.
FORENSIC-004
Automatic Containment Rule
Containment may automatically: - freeze transport - revoke connector tokens - isolate affected routes - quarantine compromised nodes - preserve replay evidence - preserve heartbeat evidence - preserve route topology - preserve event chronology Containment automatic. Authority manual.
FORENSIC-005
Manual Authority Restoration Rule
The system may NOT automatically: - restore creator authority - restart live deployment - promote replacement nodes - reconnect external routes - restore governance authority - restore archive authority Restoration requires: human verification human approval human activation
FORENSIC-006
Replacement Continuity Node Rule
If a continuity compartment fails: - damaged node remains quarantined - replay remains preserved - heartbeat evidence remains preserved - route topology remains preserved A replacement continuity node may: resume continuity elsewhere while damaged compartments remain observable. No silent destruction of evidence.

FORENSIC-008
Route Damage Evaluation
Incident review should evaluate: - affected route - affected nodes - affected replay segments - affected connectors - affected authority scopes - affected revenue routes - affected archives - affected migrations - affected audiences Damage evaluation must remain replay-visible.
FORENSIC-009
Incident Monitoring Systems
Monitoring systems may observe: - route stability - connector stability - heartbeat continuity - replay continuity - transport anomalies - geographic anomalies - authority anomalies - token anomalies - route propagation anomalies Monitoring exists for continuity protection, not hidden surveillance.
FORENSIC-010
Forensic Audit Reconstruction
A forensic audit should reconstruct: - incident origin - propagation path - affected continuity compartments - affected authority scopes - replay chronology - migration chronology - connector behavior - containment actions - recovery actions The audit reconstructs truth. The audit does not fabricate blame.
FORENSIC-011
Live Incident Visibility
Incident visibility should display: - active alarms - degraded routes - quarantined compartments - replay preservation status - heartbeat preservation status - route freeze status - connector health - authority freeze status Visibility should remain understandable during crisis.
FORENSIC-012
Emergency Stop Preservation
Universal stop command: "1" must: - stop propagation - freeze transport - preserve replay - preserve heartbeat evidence - preserve route topology - preserve forensic chronology The stop command is a continuity preservation mechanism.
FORENSIC-013
Forbidden Incident Behavior
Forbidden: silent evidence deletion Forbidden: automatic authority restoration Forbidden: hidden route continuation Forbidden: replay destruction after incident Forbidden: silent quarantine bypass Forbidden: connector reactivation without approval Forbidden: incident concealment from authorized review
FORENSIC-014
Final Rule
CyberCrowd survives incidents because continuity compartments can fail independently. A hack alarm should not collapse the entire fabric. The system: isolates preserves reroutes records and recovers while humans retain authority over restoration.
