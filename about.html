ABOUT CYBERCROWD

Built for truth, creators, governance, and continuity.

About James G. Taylor II

James G. Taylor II is the Founder and President/CEO of CyberCrowd, RETEN Records, and Atomic Kricket. He was not shaped in boardrooms, incubators, or polished mentorship circles. He was shaped in the places most systems overlook — small-town corners, outcast rooms, working-class environments, and places where talent often grows faster than opportunity.

As a kid, James built because nobody was building anything for him. Hardware, music, systems, ideas — if something could be taken apart and rebuilt stronger, he was already doing it.

That instinct became the foundation of his life: if the world does not offer a lane, build one.

RETEN Records: The First Independent Surface

RETEN Records was created as a direct response to an industry that too often overlooks creators who do not fit its mold. It was not built to impress executives. It was built to protect artists, preserve independence, and give outsiders a place to stand.

RETEN became the first proof of concept: a system can be fair, transparent, and creator-first when it is designed that way from the ground up.

Atomic Kricket: Creativity Under Pressure

Atomic Kricket was born during one of the hardest chapters of James’s life — a prison band formed inside a system built more to contain people than develop them. Inside those walls, music became clarity. Rhythm became structure. Creativity became survival.

Atomic Kricket was not just a band. It was a signal that creativity does not die under pressure — it sharpens.

That chapter taught James what no corporate environment could: how systems collapse, how people adapt, and how truth behaves when everything unnecessary is stripped away.

The Outcast Advantage

James did not return to the world trying to blend in. He returned with a clearer understanding of broken systems and the people they leave behind.

  • Outcasts see cracks before anyone else.
  • Builders see the systems behind the cracks.
  • Survivors understand the cost of ignoring them.

That perspective became the backbone of his leadership and the blueprint behind CyberCrowd.

CyberCrowd: Built Because the Old World Failed

CyberCrowd was not created to be another social platform. It was created because the existing systems were fundamentally broken.

  • Creators were unprotected.
  • Truth had no neutral ground.
  • Governance lacked transparency.
  • Platforms extracted more than they gave.
  • Communities had no continuity.
  • Outcasts had no place to belong.

CyberCrowd is the answer: a permanent doctrine-layer platform where clarity, accountability, sovereignty, and continuity are built into the architecture.

It merges every chapter of James’s life — the builder, the label founder, the prison-band survivor, the outcast, and the systems architect — into one operating environment.

A Continuity Story

This is not a comeback story. It is a continuity story.

Every chapter sharpened the next:

  • RETEN Records taught sovereignty.
  • Atomic Kricket taught resilience.
  • Outcast life taught perspective.
  • Hardware building taught precision.
  • CyberCrowd ties it all together.

CyberCrowd exists because James refused to disappear, refused to be defined by circumstance, and refused to let broken systems dictate the future.

The Mission

James builds platforms for the people who do not get invited into the room — and for the ones who never needed the invitation in the first place.

CyberCrowd is not a trend. It is not a disposable brand. It is not a startup chasing hype.

It is a permanent surface for truth, creators, governance, continuity, and operational dignity — architected by someone who lived the failures of the old world and decided to build something better.
