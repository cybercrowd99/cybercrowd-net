ABOUT CYBERCROWD

Built for truth, creators, governance, continuity, and operational resilience.

About James G. Taylor II

James G. Taylor II is the Founder and President/CEO of CyberCrowd, RETEN Records, and Atomic Kricket. He was not shaped in boardrooms, incubators, or polished mentorship circles. He was shaped in the overlooked environments where talent grows faster than opportunity and where outcasts learn to build their own systems.

As a kid, James built because nobody was building anything for him. Hardware, music, systems, ideas — if something could be taken apart and rebuilt stronger, he was already doing it.

That instinct became the foundation of his life: if the world does not offer a lane, build one.

RETEN Records

RETEN Records was created as a response to an industry that overlooked creators who did not fit its mold. It was not built to impress executives. It was built to protect artists, preserve sovereignty, and give outsiders a place to stand.

RETEN became the first proof that creator-first systems can exist when fairness and transparency are designed directly into the architecture.

Atomic Kricket

Atomic Kricket was born during one of the hardest chapters of James’s life — a prison band formed inside a system built more to contain people than develop them.

Inside those walls, music became clarity. Rhythm became structure. Creativity became survival.

Atomic Kricket was not just a band. It became proof that creativity sharpens under pressure instead of disappearing.

The Outcast Advantage

  • Outcasts see cracks before anyone else.
  • Builders see the systems behind the cracks.
  • Survivors understand the consequences of ignoring them.

That perspective became the operational philosophy behind CyberCrowd.

Why CyberCrowd Exists

CyberCrowd was not created to become another disposable social platform. It was built because existing systems failed the people using them.

  • Creators lacked protection.
  • Truth lacked neutral ground.
  • Governance lacked visibility.
  • Communities lacked continuity.
  • Platforms extracted more than they returned.
  • Outcasts had nowhere to belong.

CyberCrowd was architected as a permanent doctrine-layer environment where accountability, continuity, sovereignty, and operational visibility are built directly into the system itself.

The Mission

James builds systems for the people who never received invitations into the room — and for the people who never needed the invitation in the first place.

CyberCrowd is not a trend. It is not a hype cycle. It is not disposable infrastructure.

It is a living operational environment built for creators, governance, continuity, resilience, and truth.
